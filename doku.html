Doku

A quiet logic puzzle for iPhone. Every colored region on the board hides exactly one answer. The marks inside each region tell you where other answers may not sit. That is the entire rule set.

How it plays

A Doku board is a grid — anywhere from 4×4 to 9×9 — divided into irregular colored regions. Every square in a region carries the same small glyph, drawn as ticks radiating from an empty center. Your job is to find the one square in each region that can hold that region's answer without breaking any rule on the board.

The ticks are directions, and there are only three of them:

The rules are symmetrical — it does not matter which of two squares carries the tick, only that one of them does. Two regions can never conflict with themselves, because only one square in each region is ever the answer.

Single tap marks a square you have ruled out. Those marks are free, they cost nothing, and you can press and drag to sweep them across a whole run of squares at once. Double tap commits a square as its region's answer, and the board judges it immediately: a correct answer gains an ink frame and a center dot, a wrong one leaves a permanent red cross.

A flawless solve takes exactly one move per region, which the game calls par. In the numbered levels you get three chances; spend all three and the attempt resets, though your progress through the chapters never goes backward.

Sixty levels, six chapters

The numbered journey introduces one idea at a time, and each chapter is named for the rule it teaches. Boards start at 4×4 with eight regions and grow to 9×9 with as many as twenty-two.

Levels are laid out along a winding trail. A run of perfect solves from the first level keeps the trail gold; a single imperfect solve turns it blue for the rest of the run, which gives players who want it a reason to go back and do it properly.

A new puzzle every day

Alongside the journey there is a daily board, keyed to the calendar date on your device and rotating at local midnight. The size follows the day of the week, so the week has a shape to it: 6×6 on Monday and Tuesday, 7×7 on Wednesday and Thursday, 8×8 on Friday and Saturday, and a 9×9 on Sunday.

The daily has no chance limit. You cannot fail it — you are only ever scored on how many moves it took, which makes it a different exercise from the journey. Solving on consecutive days builds a streak, shown on the home screen as a badge that brightens as the run grows.

Fair by construction

Puzzles are generated and vetted on our own machines, never on your phone, and every one has to clear two independent tests before it is allowed into the app.

The first is a solver that searches exhaustively and confirms the board has exactly one valid solution. The second is a deduction engine that replays the board the way a person would, using only three techniques the game actually teaches: confirming a region when it has one candidate left, eliminating candidates that a confirmed answer forbids, and ruling out a candidate whose choice would starve some other region of every option. If the engine cannot finish the board with those three moves, the puzzle is discarded.

The practical consequence is the only promise that matters in a logic game: you are never required to guess. Every board can be reasoned to the end, and how hard a board is graded on how much of that harder third technique it demands.

Details we cared about

Light and dark

A warm paper light theme and a deep, calm dark theme, switched in settings. Region colors are chosen so that neighboring regions land far apart in perceptual color space rather than just looking different on a designer's monitor.

Readable at any size

Three text sizes — small, medium and large — and every screen in the app responds to the setting immediately, not just the board.

Color is never the information

The ink glyphs always carry the rule, so color only groups regions. Players who want a further cue can turn on region tags, which letters every region and strengthens its boundary.

Sound without sound files

The app ships no audio assets at all. Its tones are synthesized as they play — correct answers walk up a rising triad, and a wrong guess sends the phrase back to the start. Sound and haptics can each be turned off.

Learn by playing

An interactive tutorial teaches the rules on real boards rather than in a wall of text, and it can be skipped or replayed at any time. A rules sheet is one tap away from every board.

Never loses your place

An in-progress board is written to disk on every move. Close the app mid-puzzle, come back a day later, and the board is exactly where you left it.
